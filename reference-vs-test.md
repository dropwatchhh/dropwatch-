# Reference videos vs. my sprite test

What I looked at: contact sheets and full frames from the reference account's videos (Days 1, 4, 5, 9, 11 in detail, plus the hook, middle and ending sheets of the others). This is study only. Nothing below is reused: my characters, story, setting, scripts and voice stay original.

## What the reference videos actually do

| Area | What the frames show |
|---|---|
| **Body** | Full body is on screen in most shots (head to shoes) in wide and medium shots. Close-ups are used for punchlines only (big open-mouth faces, a hand holding a bag to the face). |
| **Arms and hands** | Arms and hands are always attached and always acting: holding a phone up to the camera, a bag of chips to the face, a fork, an arm thrown up in a startle, hands on hips, pointing, a foot lifted off the floor. Props are in the hands. |
| **Camera** | A different angle about every 1.6–2 seconds: high angle looking down at a bed, low angle from behind, a side profile at street level, over-the-shoulder, a close-up on a face, a macro on a prop, and wide establishing shots. Slow pushes and tilts happen inside shots. |
| **Backgrounds** | Fully built sets with depth: a wood-paneled basement with a bed and PC, a 70s kitchen with wallpaper and a checkered tablecloth, a gym with racks and dumbbells, a gas station with pumps, cars and blue sky, a suburban street with houses, a fence and a hydrant, a store with fridges and shelves. |
| **Props** | Every scene has set dressing and key props (a digital clock reading 3:03 AM, boxes, a lamp, a cockroach on the counter, a stack of cash). Small labels with leader lines name them ("RENT: $0", "TINY TREES"). |
| **Lighting and shadows** | Simple, clean, bright game-style 3D: saturated colors, soft contact shadows under characters and props, warm lamps indoors, flat daylight outdoors, a neon pink/blue night look in the opening. Nothing floats. |
| **Motion** | Constant body animation: walking, climbing stairs, arm swings, startles, idle sway. Shots are short, with fades and flashes between scenes. |
| **Overlays** | A permanent HUD in the top-left, real viewer comment cards (username, day tag, like count), labels with leader lines, word-by-word captions in the lower third. |
| **Expressions** | Big, readable cartoon expressions with body language to match, and often a different expression every shot. |
| **Pacing** | 30–37 cuts a minute in the middle of the series (a shot every 1.6–2 s). |

## What my sprite test did instead

| Area | My test |
|---|---|
| Body | A floating bust. No arms, no hands, no torso below the chest, no legs. |
| Arms and hands | None. |
| Camera | One fixed front view with a slow zoom for 5 seconds. |
| Background | A flat purple gradient. No ground, no objects. |
| Props | None. |
| Lighting and shadows | Nice studio lighting on the face, but he floats with no ground and no shadow on anything. |
| Motion | Face layers swapped and the whole picture bobbed. The body itself never moved. |
| Overlays | Captions only. No LIVE badge, count or comments. |
| Expressions | Real, but subtle and face-only. |
| Pacing | One continuous shot. |

## Can sprites close the gap? No.

Layered sprites are flat pictures. They can't give a full body whose arms and hands bend, hold props and reach, a camera that moves to a new angle every 2 seconds, or shadows that match from shot to shot. Each new angle or pose would need its own set of renders. A real 3D character in a 3D scene is needed.
