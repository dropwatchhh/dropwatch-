# Style report: @kevinthechud

Study only. Nothing from these videos (characters, scripts, jokes, footage, audio, voice, name) is reused.

## What I analyzed

All 11 videos the account had public when I looked (2026-11-27): the reference video (Day 1, 1.5M views) and Days 2–11 (about 185K–315K views each). The full account fits inside the "up to 10 others" limit, so there was no need to sample.

Each video got: frames and contact sheets, a local whisper.cpp transcript (`base` model), and an FFmpeg pass for cuts, silences and loudness. TikTok did not block the listing or the downloads, and I used no login or cookies.

How closely I looked:

- **Frames viewed by me:** Day 1 (hook sheet, middle sheets and ending sheets), plus the opening and ending sheets of Day 9 and the ending sheets of Days 10 and 11.
- **Transcript and FFmpeg data only:** Days 2–8, which I did not view frame by frame. Claims about their visuals are inferred from the recurring pattern, and I mark them as such.

## Posting cadence (measured from upload timestamps)

About one episode per day (11 episodes in 10 days; gaps of 18–30 hours). Upload times drifted between 17:35 and 03:51 UTC, so there is no fixed slot. The first video earned 1.5M views, and later episodes held a steady 185K–315K. The newest (Day 11) was the best of the follow-ups at about 315K.

- **Number of choices:** 9 of 11 videos offer exactly 2 options. Two use an open "most liked comment" prompt. None offers 3.
- **Choices are one to three words.** They read as a concrete action ("his teeth", "a job"), never a vague mood.
- **Rule stated aloud:** "most votes wins" or "most likes wins" (the two are used interchangeably, so the rule is slightly unclear).
- **Voice:** the same narrator voice delivers the question. There is no change of tone that I could detect from the transcript.

### On-screen treatment (frames)

- **Day 1:** a split-screen card, blue on one side and green on the other, with the same character holding a different prop on each side. A yellow "COMMENT ONE WORD" banner, the two option labels with one-line jokes under them, a yellow "VOTE NOW" button, and "KEVIN WILL DO WHAT YOU PICK" below.
- **Days 9–11:** the same split-screen idea with a green "DAY N: YOU PICK" banner, labeled A and B circles, a short description under each option, and "COMMENT A OR B / MOST VOTES WINS" at the bottom.
- **The card builds in.** On Day 11 the banner appears first (about 0:55.9), then the A option (0:58), then the B option and the bottom banner (1:00). This gives the viewer a reason to stay to the last second.

### Timing

The spoken question starts 4–11 seconds before the end (typically 6–8 s), and the visual card is on screen for the last 3–8 seconds. The question is the last thing said, so it is the final thing viewers hear before the loop restarts.

### Does the next episode follow the winning comment?

**Yes, every time, as far as the videos show.** In all 10 follow-up episodes, the opening words are "You voted…" and the episode then does one of the options from the previous ending:

| Ending offered | Next episode opens |

| Day 2: open (what else to fix) | Day 3: "You voted. Seventeen thousand of you. Kevin is getting a haircut." |

Details worth copying in spirit:

- **Vote counts are quoted aloud** (17,000, 14,000, 6,000, 10,000). That makes viewers feel counted.
- **Real viewer comments appear as on-screen cards** in the first seconds. On Day 9 a comment card with a commenter handle, a day tag and a like count appears at about 2 s and another at about 6.7 s. The second one (a request about the cockroach having its own terrarium, 146 likes) is then granted in the same episode. So viewers see their *own* ideas, not only the winning option.
- **A promised, un-voted item can be delivered quietly.** Day 5 says "new clothes… that's tomorrow, no vote needed", and Day 6 delivers it inside a different vote's episode.

**What I could not verify:** whether the option that was announced as the winner really had the most votes. I did not read the comment sections, and the vote counts are the creator's own claims. All I can say is that the episodes consistently follow one of the offered choices.

## Style findings

### Hook (first 3 seconds)

- **Day 1:** a scene with no cut for 6.7 seconds: feet under a glowing blanket, with the captioned line "Three a.m. two people under one blanket." It is atmospheric and curious, but slow compared with later episodes.
- **Days 2–11:** every follow-up opens with the voice saying "You voted…" within the first second. The hook is the payoff of the previous vote, and for returning viewers it is a promise kept.
- **Gap I see:** for a brand-new viewer, "You voted clean" means nothing. The account relies on returning viewers, and only Day 1 works as a cold-start hook.

### Story structure (repeated in every follow-up)

1. Recap the vote (about 0–5 s).
2. The task, with a comic complication in the form of a recurring side character (about 5–20 s).
3. Three to four fast gag beats, escalating (about 20–45 s).
4. Result and a visible stat change (about 45–55 s).
5. Teaser and vote (last 5–10 s).

The humor leans heavily on mocking a character's body, skin and hygiene. It works for this account, but it is a choice you don't have to follow, and it is something platforms and advertisers can react to.

### Length

61–76 seconds (median about 65). Days 2–11 have no silence of any length (one or two gaps of under a second at most), so the narration runs continuously from start to finish.

### Camera framing

Mostly a medium or close shot of the character, with frequent punch-in close-ups on the face for a punchline. Wide shots establish a new location. Phone and chat screens are shown full-frame for text-message moments. This is from the frames I viewed (Days 1, 9, 10 and 11).

### Art style

3D cartoon characters with exaggerated proportions and game-like lighting (a pink glow at night, warm wood tones by day). A recurring cast: a main character, a grandmother (round glasses, white bun), a pet cockroach, a gas-station clerk, and a girlfriend shown only through a chat screen.

### Captions

Word-by-word, one to three words at a time, centered in the lower third and synced to the voice. White bold text with a dark outline, and one keyword in a bright color (yellow, red, blue, pink or green). Roughly 6 words per second of speech.

Extra graphics: a permanent counter at top left (a "days left" number and a red percentage meter that falls over the series), and small labels with arrows pointing at things ("RENT: $0", "TENANTS", "BRACES (2ND TIME)").

### Voice

Third-person, present-tense narration in short, deadpan sentences, with a punchline in the last two to four words ("Those aren't stains. That's Takis."). The transcripts cannot tell me whether it is a human voice or text-to-speech.

### Music and sound effects

**Not judged.** FFmpeg shows steady loudness of about -14 to -15 LUFS (loudness range 3.4–4.9 LU) and no silence, which means there is always sound, but I cannot separate voice from music or effects. Please listen to one episode and tell me about it (see the questions below).

### Ending

An end card (see above), with the narrator saying the question last. There is no sign-off, so the loop restarts straight into the next play.

### How the videos appear to be made

I cannot tell. The look is consistent with a 3D game engine or 3D animation software, and also with AI-generated 3D-style clips; nothing in the frames or audio settles it. I'm not guessing further.
